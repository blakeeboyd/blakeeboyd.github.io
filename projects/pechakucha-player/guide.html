PechaKucha Player Guide

A web-based presentation tool with automatic slide timing

What is PechaKucha?

PechaKucha (Japanese for "chit-chat") is a presentation format created by Klein-Dytham Architecture in Tokyo in 2003. The classic format uses 20 slides shown for 20 seconds each, totaling 6 minutes and 40 seconds.

The key constraint: slides auto-advance whether the speaker is ready or not. This forces concise storytelling and keeps presentations energetic. The format has spread to over 1,000 cities worldwide.

This tool lets you create and practice PechaKucha-style presentations with customizable timing, speaker notes, and two presentation modes.

Getting Started

To create a presentation:

  1. Upload images by dragging them into the dropzone or clicking "Choose Files"
  2. Organize slides by dragging thumbnails to reorder them
  3. Add speaker notes by clicking any slide to select it
  4. Choose your mode (Practice or Present)
  5. Start presenting with the Start button

File Naming

Name your image files with numbers (1.jpg, 2.jpg, 3.jpg...) for automatic sorting. The player uses natural sorting, so "2.jpg" will come before "10.jpg".

Supported Formats

The player accepts any image format your browser supports: JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, and SVG.

Organizing Slides

Reordering

Drag any thumbnail to a new position. A blue indicator shows where the slide will be inserted when you drop it.

Deleting Slides

Hover over a thumbnail to reveal the delete button (X) in the top-right corner. Click it to remove the slide. You can undo deletions with Ctrl/Cmd + Z or by clicking "Undo" in the toast notification.

Adding More Slides

Click the "+ Add more" card at the end of your slides, or drag additional images anywhere on the page to add them to your presentation.

Preview Slides

Hover over a thumbnail and click the eye icon (top-left) to view a full-size preview. Press Esc or click anywhere to close the preview.

Title Slide

You can designate one slide as a title slide that displays before the timed presentation begins. The title slide does not count against your presentation time.

To set a title slide:

  1. Click any slide thumbnail to select it
  2. Click "Set as Title Slide" in the notes panel

The title slide appears in the dedicated slot on the left side of the slide grid. Click the X on the title slide thumbnail to remove it and return the slide to the main sequence.

Speaker Notes

Click any slide to open the notes panel where you can:

  • Add notes: Type your talking points for this slide
  • Set custom duration: Override the default timing for this specific slide
  • Set as title slide: Designate this slide as the title

Slides with notes show a small blue dot in the bottom-right corner of their thumbnail.

Slides with custom durations show a small orange dot in the top-right corner.

Tip

Keep notes brief. The auto-advancing format means you only have seconds per slide, so bullet points work better than paragraphs.

Presentation Modes

The player offers two modes for different situations:

Practice Mode

A single-screen fullscreen view ideal for rehearsing. The presentation shows on your main screen with optional overlays:

  • Timer overlay: Large countdown numbers on the slide (toggle with the Timer checkbox)
  • Notes panel: Your speaker notes appear in a panel on the right (toggle with the Notes checkbox)
  • Audio cues: Beeps at 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 seconds remaining (toggle with the Audio cues checkbox)

Use the navigation arrows or ←/→ keys to move between slides manually.

Present Mode

Opens a separate window for your audience while you see a presenter view. Best used with two screens (your laptop and a projector/external monitor).

The presenter view shows:

  • Current slide: What your audience sees now
  • Next slide: Preview of what's coming
  • Timer: Large countdown with color warnings (yellow at 5s, red at 3s, pulsing at 1s)
  • Elapsed/total time: Track your overall progress
  • Notes: Editable speaker notes (changes save automatically)
  • Filmstrip: Clickable thumbnails for quick navigation
Tip

Position your presenter view on your laptop and drag the audience window to your external display. The audience window can be made fullscreen with F11 (or Ctrl+Cmd+F on Mac).

Timing Settings

Global Duration

The default slide duration is 20 seconds (classic PechaKucha timing). You can change this in the Settings panel before starting. Common alternatives:

  • 15 seconds: Faster pace, 5-minute presentation for 20 slides
  • 20 seconds: Classic PechaKucha (6:40 for 20 slides)
  • 30 seconds: Slower pace, good for complex visuals

Per-Slide Duration

Override the timing for individual slides in the notes panel. This is useful when:

  • A complex diagram needs more time
  • A transition slide only needs a few seconds
  • You want to build in pauses for audience reaction

Image Display Modes

Control how images appear on screen:

  • Fit: Shows the entire image with black bars if the aspect ratio doesn't match the screen
  • Fill: Fills the entire screen, cropping edges if necessary
  • Native: Shows the image at its original pixel size, centered on a black background

For most presentations, Fit is recommended to ensure nothing gets cropped.

Export and Import

Exporting

Click "Export Settings" to download a JSON file containing:

  • Slide order (by filename)
  • Speaker notes for each slide
  • Per-slide duration overrides
  • Title slide designation
  • Global settings

The export does not include the images themselves, only references to their filenames.

Importing

To restore a presentation:

  1. Upload your images (use the same filenames as before)
  2. Upload the JSON file along with the images, or use "Import Settings"

The player matches slides by filename, so keep your original filenames when re-importing.

Tip

Export your presentation before important events. If anything goes wrong, you can quickly restore your slide order and notes.

During Presentation

Pausing

Press Space to pause the auto-advance timer. Press again to resume. A "Paused" indicator appears while paused.

Manual Navigation

Use ← and → arrow keys to move between slides. In presenter mode, you can also click thumbnails in the filmstrip or use the Prev/Next buttons.

Exiting

Press Esc to exit the presentation and return to the editor. In presenter mode, click "Edit Slides" to return while keeping your audience window open.

Completion

After the last slide, a completion screen shows your total slide count and presentation time. From here you can:

  • Present Again: Restart from the beginning
  • Edit Slides: Return to the editor
  • New Presentation: Clear everything and start fresh

Tips for Great PechaKucha Presentations

  • Practice until automatic: Run through your presentation multiple times until you can deliver each slide's content naturally within the time limit
  • Let images tell the story: Avoid text-heavy slides. The format rewards visual storytelling over bullet points
  • Plan your transitions: Know exactly what you'll say as each slide appears. The auto-advance waits for no one
  • Embrace the constraint: The time limit keeps energy high and prevents rambling. Trust the format
  • Use the audio cues: The countdown beeps help you feel the rhythm without constantly watching the timer
  • Test your setup: Before presenting, verify your images display correctly and the timing feels right
