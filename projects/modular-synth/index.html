Background

A modular synthesizer generates sound by connecting discrete modules together with patch cables. Each module does one thing: an oscillator generates a waveform, an amplifier controls volume, and so on. The power comes from the connections between them.

This approach differs from a fixed-architecture synthesizer where the signal path is predetermined. In a modular system, you decide how audio flows by creating the connections yourself.

The four modules here represent the minimum chain needed to produce and hear a sound: a VCO (Voltage Controlled Oscillator) generates the raw waveform, a VCA (Voltage Controlled Amplifier) controls its level, the Scope lets you see the waveform shape, and the Output sends the signal to your speakers.

Exercise

  1. See the waveform: Drag from the VCO's output port to the Scope's input port. A waveform should appear on the scope display. Try switching between sine, sawtooth, square, and triangle waveforms to see how the shape changes.
  2. Hear the sound: Connect VCO Out to VCA In, then VCA Out to Output In. You should hear the oscillator through your speakers. Start with the Output volume low and adjust up to a comfortable level.
  3. Explore waveforms: With both the Scope and Output connected, switch between waveforms. Listen to how each sounds different while observing how the visual shape corresponds to the timbre you hear. A sine wave is smooth and pure, a sawtooth is bright and buzzy, a square is hollow, and a triangle is soft.
  4. Change the pitch: Drag the Coarse knob up and down to sweep across frequencies (20 Hz to 2000 Hz). Click the value display to toggle between Hz and note name. Use the Fine knob for small adjustments measured in cents, where 100 cents equals one semitone.
