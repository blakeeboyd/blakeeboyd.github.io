What is PechaKucha?

PechaKucha (ぺちゃくちゃ, Japanese for "chit-chat") is a presentation format created by Klein-Dytham Architecture in Tokyo in 2003. The classic format uses 20 slides shown for 20 seconds each, totaling 6 minutes and 40 seconds. Visit klein-dytham.com/pechakucha to learn more about the format's history and find PechaKucha events near you.

The key constraint: slides auto-advance whether the speaker is ready or not. This forces concise storytelling and keeps presentations energetic. The format has spread to over 1,000 cities worldwide.

How to Use This Tool

  1. Upload images by dragging them into the dropzone or clicking "Choose Files." Name files in numerical order (1.jpg, 2.jpg...) for automatic sorting.
  2. Organize your slides by dragging thumbnails to reorder. Click a slide to select it and add speaker notes.
  3. Set a title slide (optional) by selecting a slide and clicking "Set as Title Slide." This will display first before the timed presentation begins.
  4. Choose your mode: Practice shows the presentation fullscreen on a single screen, while Present opens a separate window for your audience and gives you presenter view with notes, timer, and slide navigation.
  5. Start presenting! Slides will auto-advance according to your timing settings.

Keyboard Shortcuts

During presentation: Space pauses/resumes the timer. Esc exits the slideshow. ← and → navigate between slides.

In preview: Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes the last deleted slide.

Tips for Great PechaKucha Presentations

  • Practice until you can deliver your talk within the time constraint without rushing.
  • Let images tell the story. Avoid text-heavy slides; the format rewards visual storytelling.
  • Plan your transitions. Know exactly what you'll say as each slide appears.
  • Embrace the constraint. The auto-advance keeps energy high and prevents rambling.

Export and Import

Use the Export button to save your presentation as a JSON file, including slide order, speaker notes, and settings. This lets you back up your work or share it with others.

To restore a presentation, upload your images along with the exported JSON file. The tool will automatically detect the JSON and apply your saved slide order, notes, and settings. Note that images are stored by filename, so keep the same filenames when re-importing.
