Is doubling speakers +3dB or +6dB? What's parallel and series wiring?

Takeaway

Sheets opens with a poll showing nobody knows whether adding a second subwoofer raises output by 3 dB or 6 dB, then settles it by measurement. He runs a 36 Hz tone into a single 16 ohm sub, hooks a voltmeter and a current meter directly to the amp's output, and watches the numbers as he rewires the cabinets. Parallel: voltage stays at 20 V, current doubles to 2 A, load drops to 8 ohms, microphone reads +6 dB. Series: voltage and current at the amp output stay the same, but voltage splits between the two speakers, load doubles to 32 ohms, microphone reads no change. The closing impedance sweep makes the underlying principle visible: amplifier current isn't constant across frequency because speaker impedance isn't constant, and a well-designed cabinet flattens the variation.

  • Familiarity with Ohm's law (V = IR) at the level of a high-school physics class
  • Comfortable with the idea that decibels are logarithmic
  • Watch Loudspeaker basics in under ten minutes! first for basic speaker construction

By the end you'll be able to

  • Distinguish parallel and series wiring at the terminal level
  • Predict how amplifier voltage, current, and load change for each configuration

  • Read a speaker impedance curve and explain why amplifier current rises and falls across frequency
