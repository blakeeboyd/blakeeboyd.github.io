Takeaway

Dennis asks how to save time in tuning. Joel asks the same question in different words: with limited quiet time, usually shared with lighting focus or scenic artists, how do you triage? Lively answers Dennis's question with the answer to Joel's, then defines the borrowed medical term. Triage assigns urgency to wounds in a disaster, so the engineer treats the largest problems first instead of trying to fix everything at once. Lively's heuristic is that the largest problems in most rooms sit in speaker placement and aim, so those are what he checks first.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Familiarity with the basic stages of system tuning (placement, aim, EQ, delay)
  • Awareness that production schedules often share quiet time across departments

By the end you'll be able to

  • Define triage as it applies to system tuning under time pressure
  • Justify checking speaker placement and aim before reaching for EQ when time is short

Watch for

  • 1:12 the answer lands: go for the biggest problems first, and most of them probably sit in speaker placement and aim
