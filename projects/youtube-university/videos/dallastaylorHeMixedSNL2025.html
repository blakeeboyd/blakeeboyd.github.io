Takeaway

Bob Palladino, who mixed Saturday Night Live for 40 years, gives Dallas Taylor a tour of the SNL audio desk during his final week on the show. He walks through the RF and boom microphone layout, the 25 to 30 audience mics, the foot-pedal applause control he calls his "secret weapon," and the color-coded script book where last-minute changes between dress rehearsal and air get tracked in red ink. Halfway through, Fred Hedemark introduces himself as the incoming A1 and reflects on the trajectory from MSNBC to Late Night to the SNL chair. The video closes with the final mix of season 50 and Palladino's farewell to his crew.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • General awareness of broadcast TV production
  • Familiarity with the concept of an audio mixing console
  • No prior SNL knowledge needed

By the end you'll be able to

  • Describe the role of an A1 (dialogue) mixer on a live broadcast show
  • Identify the signal sources Palladino routes (RFs, booms, audience mics, playback and SFX rooms)
  • Explain why an applause foot pedal lets the mixer write applause without taking hands off other controls
  • Articulate how the SNL workflow handles last-minute script changes between dress and air

Watch for

  • 5:30 the foot-pedal applause control Palladino calls his 'secret weapon'
  • 7:09 the audience mic count: between 25 and 30 distributed mics
  • 10:30 the color-coded script book with red-ink late changes between dress and air
