How MLB is mixed live inside a truck

Dallas Taylor · 12:08 · Applied

Broadcast / Sports Broadcast

Watch on YouTube ↗

Takeaway

Glenn Stilwell, six-time Emmy A1 mixer, gives Dallas Taylor a tour of the broadcast audio truck for a Washington Nationals game. He covers the 72-fader Calrec Artemis console with its GPI-triggered automation, the parabolic dishes that capture the bat crack (delayed eight frames to sync with the on-screen strike-zone graphic), the patching workflow from mult sheets into console inputs, his "EQ is for solving problems" approach to layering crowd at 2 kHz, specialty mics at 8 kHz, and voice around 4 kHz, and his simultaneous monitoring across stereo, 5.1, and 5.1.4 Atmos out of one set of Neumann speakers. He closes with his view that stereo is the wrong format for broadcast sports because there is no room for the mix to breathe.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • General awareness of how TV broadcasts are produced
  • Familiarity with the concept of mixing audio levels
  • No prior broadcast audio experience required

By the end you'll be able to

  • Explain the purpose of a mix minus and what would go wrong without one
