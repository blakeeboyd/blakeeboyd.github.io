Takeaway

Worrall opens with a surprising fact about microphone construction: dynamic mics are velocity transducers (they measure air movement), while condenser mics are pressure transducers (essentially high-resolution barometers). That means even when their capsules are placed at exactly the same distance from a source, the two outputs are 90° out of phase. This is not a timing difference, so there's no comb filtering, but it also can't be corrected by sliding one mic forward or back, since adjusting distance just adds a frequency-dependent shift on top. From there, Worrall pivots into a broader argument against pedants who insist "phase is just timing": he derives all 360° of phase shift from a single sine wave in Reaktor using only polarity inversion, a one-sample rate-of-change calculation, and a four-input scanner crossfade. His refrain: delay always means phase shift, but phase shift does not always mean delay.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Watch Delay Always Means Phase Shift But Phase Shift Does Not Always Mean Delay first if you haven't
  • Familiarity with dynamic and condenser microphone types
  • Comfortable with the idea of pressure vs. velocity in physics

By the end you'll be able to

  • Distinguish dynamic mics (velocity transducers) from condenser mics (pressure transducers)
  • Explain why these mic types are 90° out of phase even at the same distance from a source
  • Argue that phase is not always about timing
  • Derive a 90° phase shift from a sine wave using only polarity, a one-sample delta, and a crossfade
