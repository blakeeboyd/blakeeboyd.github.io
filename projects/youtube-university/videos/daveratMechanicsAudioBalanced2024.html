The Mechanics Of Audio Balanced Lines Explained

Dave Rat · 05:50 · Foundational

Takeaway

Rat builds a hand-made tabletop demo, three colored bars representing ground, pin 2, and pin 3, with real wires running to a working preamp and oscilloscope, to make balanced audio cabling physically visible. The mic coil generates a voltage that pushes pin 2 and pin 3 equal-and-opposite while ground sits at 0 volts; the receiving preamp inverts one polarity and adds them, doubling the signal. Rat detaches the ground bar entirely to drive home that ground is a reference, not a conductor in the signal path. He then collapses the demo into an unbalanced circuit by shorting pin 3 to ground; the scope shows one trace double in amplitude while the other goes flat. The closing move is the payoff: any noise induced onto the cable along its run lands on both conductors the same, not equal-and-opposite. The same subtractive preamp that doubles the differential signal cancels the common-mode noise.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Familiar with what a microphone signal is and the existence of XLR cables
  • Awareness that audio cables can pick up hum and interference
  • Some exposure to a mixing console input or DI box

By the end you'll be able to

  • Describe how a microphone coil produces equal-and-opposite voltages on pin 2 and pin 3
  • Explain why a balanced line works even with ground disconnected from the signal path
  • Distinguish what the preamp's subtractive sum does to differential signal vs. common-mode noise
  • Predict the preamp output when noise is induced equally onto both conductors of a balanced cable
