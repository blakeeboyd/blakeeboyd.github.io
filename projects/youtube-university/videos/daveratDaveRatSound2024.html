Takeaway

Rat lays out a sound-check strategy designed to maximize the artist's productive stage time and minimize wasted minutes on mic-by-mic line checks that don't reflect anything that will happen during the show. He names six possible scenarios (from "all off" to "all mics, all instruments") and argues that the common practice (one mic at a time with everything else muted) produces information about a state that never recurs once the band starts playing. His preferred method is an active sound check: the artist rehearses songs while the engineer brings up instruments in context, EQ'ing each one while the bleed from neighboring instruments and the resonance of the stage are already happening. He also gives a practical rule for handling a broken channel: don't halt the sound check, keep moving, and fix it during set change.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Familiarity with the concept of soundcheck and line check
  • Awareness that mic bleed affects how channels sound in a real mix
  • Some exposure to live mixing workflow

By the end you'll be able to

  • Identify the six soundcheck scenarios and what each reveals
  • Explain why one-mic-one-instrument tests produce information about a state that never recurs
  • Run an active soundcheck workflow with the band playing throughout
  • Apply the 'keep moving, fix later' rule to broken channels during limited stage time
