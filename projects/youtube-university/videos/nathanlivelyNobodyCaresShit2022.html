Takeaway

A long-form conversation with Sean Sullivan, a touring FOH engineer with 26 years of experience mixing arena and stadium acts. The title comes from the Radiohead Coachella 2017 incident: nobody wants to pay for a backup console until the main one crashes and the band walks off stage. Sullivan uses that as the anchor for a wider argument about posture: build a career through desire and capability rather than rate negotiation, mix huge rather than loud so the show translates and so your hearing survives, tune to material relevant to tonight's show, and know the gear you spec instead of hoping the system engineer will cover for you. He also walks through his actual workflow: virtual sound check via Pro Tools, sine-sweep alignment between zones for better FFT coherence, near-field translation at home with Genelec 8040s, and a Waves LV1 wired in parallel to his Avid S6L so he can push one fader and keep the show going while his system engineer reboots the main desk.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Familiarity with the role of a front-of-house engineer at a touring show
  • Awareness that digital consoles can and do crash
  • Some exposure to live mixing or live event production

By the end you'll be able to

  • Articulate what a minimum-viable backup plan looks like and why it pays for itself even when never used
  • Identify the career posture Sullivan recommends to engineers starting out: capability before compensation
  • Distinguish 'mixing huge' from 'mixing loud' and explain why the distinction matters for hearing longevity
  • Place Sullivan's gig-prep workflow (near-field translation, virtual sound check, sine sweep alignment) in relation to your own
