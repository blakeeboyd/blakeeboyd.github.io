Should You Tune Your Live Sound System Flat?

Michael Curtis · 18:53 · Applied

Live Sound / System Tuning, Acoustics · Audio Theory / System Tuning, Acoustics

eqroom-acoustics

Watch on YouTube ↗

Takeaway

Curtis argues against flat system tuning and walks through a target curve workflow. He defines what "flat" actually means (every frequency equally represented on a transfer function measurement), introduces Michael Lawrence's target curve (a +15 dB low-end bump from 300 Hz to 63 Hz, a 1 kHz unity point, a ~1.5 dB downward tilt above), and explains why rooms naturally tilt low end up because long wavelengths don't steer from waveguides. He closes with a reference-track playlist for ear-checking the tune (Phoenix by Andrew Holmes for kick/snare contrast, Vultures by John Mayer for swimmy rooms, Get Lucky by Daft Punk for top-end clarity) and a psychoacoustic argument that the system should not have the same response front to back because the brain expects more top end at close range.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Basic familiarity with EQ and system tuning concepts
  • Awareness of measurement software like Smaart or Open Sound Meter (helpful but not required)
  • Some exposure to live sound mixing

By the end you'll be able to

  • Distinguish flat tuning from target-curve tuning and explain why flat fails in live contexts
  • Explain why rooms naturally build up low frequencies and how line arrays amplify the effect
  • Use a multi-track reference playlist to ear-check different frequency-band failures
  • Justify why a system should not be tuned to the same response front to back
