Takeaway

Curtis covers EQ for system engineers, structured around Pat Brown's "paintbrush" workflow: start with one-octave banding and a wide brush, then move to one-third-octave, then one-sixth-octave, getting progressively narrower in scope. Curtis explains the distinction between speaker EQ (manufacturer-applied, flat target, anechoic) and system EQ (engineer-applied in the room, target-curve based) and then tackles the "phase boogeyman" with calm math: minimum-phase EQ does change phase, but the shift is calculable and small at typical move sizes (~30° for a +6 dB shelf), and equal-and-opposite filters sum to zero (complementary EQ). He closes by sorting common acoustic phenomena into EQ-solvable (low-mid buildup, with limits) and not-EQ-solvable (room modes, floor bounces, SBIR), with practical alternatives like moving subs against walls to eliminate boundary interference.
