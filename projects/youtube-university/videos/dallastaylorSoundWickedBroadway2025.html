Takeaway

Josh Maszle, head of audio for Wicked on Broadway, gives a tour of the rig and his mixing approach. He covers the hidden Meyer PA built around the goal of making the show sound sourced on stage rather than reinforced, the 36-pack Countryman B6 setup with redundant rigs for principals, the 23-piece orchestra mic'd "like an orchestra" with air between sources, and the 16-mix Aviom personal monitor system for the pit. He works on a Cadac J-Type, one of the last analog consoles on Broadway, using VCAs and MIDI-triggered Kontakt cues for sound effects. He closes by contrasting his first show (sweaty palms, just hit the right faders at the right time) with his current approach (anticipating what each performer needs, second by second).

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Familiarity with musical theater conventions
  • Awareness that wireless mics use radio frequencies and need coordination
  • Basic console knowledge (faders, VCAs, scenes)

By the end you'll be able to

  • Describe how a Broadway sound designer aims for sound sourced on stage
  • Compare trade-offs between analog and digital consoles for live mixing
  • Explain why an orchestra is mic'd 'like an orchestra' rather than close-mic'd
  • Describe the difference between a new engineer's mixing and an experienced engineer's mixing

Questions

What workflow choices does Maszle describe (mic placement, foldback policy, deck speakers) that support this aesthetic, and what would change if the show prioritized maximum clarity instead?Analyze
  2. 2.Maszle still mixes on a Cadac J-Type analog console while most Broadway shows have moved to digital. What does he gain by staying analog, what does he give up, and how do features like VCAs and MIDI-triggered Kontakt cues compensate for what a digital console would do automatically?Evaluate
  3. 3.The orchestra is mic'd "like an orchestra, not like a band" with each mic capturing a little air and bleed from neighboring instruments. Why is this approach used for Wicked, and what mixing problems does it create compared to close-mic'd, isolated tracks?Analyze
