Takeaway

Continuation of the panning demo. After re-summarizing the four techniques from part one (level, Haas/delay, EQ, compression), Rat adds polarity reversal and immediately warns against using it as a panning tool. He then combines delay, compression, and EQ on a single side to create stereo imaging without altering level, which is what a live engineer wants in a venue where audience members can rarely hear both PA hangs equally. The Rats wander into perceptual territory: the 30 ms separation threshold above which the brain stops fusing two arrivals into a single source, reverb-panning as another technique, and how live sound and audiology speak about similar phenomena in incompatible vocabularies.
