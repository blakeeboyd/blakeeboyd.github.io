Takeaway

Rat walks through how he sets gain structure on a console and through the signal chain. He names four constraints: ergonomics (where the knobs sit physically), visuals (whether the fader positions logically reflect the mix), noise floor at the bottom, and distortion at the top. His method is to drive each input until the clip light triggers, back off a couple of clicks, set the master output near 0 dB, and then adjust the amplifier (not the console) to bring the room to volume. The underlying principle is "send hot, turn down late": push a strong signal as far down the chain as possible so that any noise picked up along the way gets attenuated together with the signal at the final stage. He closes with the goal that the fader layout should visually match what you are hearing: vocals on top, kick hot, snare back.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Familiarity with mixing console basics (gain, fader, output)
  • Awareness of the signal chain from input to amp to speaker
  • Some exposure to clip lights and headroom

By the end you'll be able to

  • Set gain structure to balance ergonomics, visuals, noise floor, and distortion
  • Apply the 'send hot, turn down late' principle through the signal chain
  • Use dynamic signals (kick drum) rather than continuous tones for initial gain setting
  • Design a fader visual layout that matches the mix's audible balance
