12 Months of Gigs Taught Me This About PA Setup

Silver Creek Audio · ~15:30 · Reflective

Watch on YouTube ↗

Takeaway

Connor has run sound at one Texas venue for a year, and this video walks the PA setup while cataloguing what evolved: QSC K12.2s replacing the EV ZLX-12Ps, low-profile flex stands replacing bulky tripod stands, a side-mounted console instead of center stage, short DIY power cables, an added front fill, and a lighting rig that went through three failed iterations before settling. He narrates each decision against the gig that prompted it, so the through-line is the reasoning: rental availability, stage tidiness, what a solo artist needs. The standout claim is that lighting was the single biggest improvement over the year, outweighing any audio upgrade. It is a practitioner's revision log, useful precisely because it shows which first guesses were wrong.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Basic familiarity with PA components: speakers, stands, monitors, console
  • Some sense of what a small live gig involves
  • Helpful to have seen the earlier Silver Creek video on a single gig

By the end you'll be able to

  • Trace how a small PA setup evolves under repeated real-world use
  • Explain gear-selection reasoning beyond raw sound quality, including rental availability and transport
  • Evaluate why a lighting upgrade can outweigh audio upgrades in perceived production value
  • Question default setup choices when the gig is smaller than the rig was designed for

Watch for

  • 1:17 the QSC K12.2 vs EV ZLX-12P swap, decided mostly on rental availability rather than sound
  • 4:05 the realization that one floor monitor works fine for a solo artist placed center stage
  • 9:58 Connor names lighting the single biggest improvement to the whole PA setup over the year
