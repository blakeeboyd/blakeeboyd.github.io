Takeaway

A 4-minute primer on the two specs that decide whether an amplifier suits your speakers: impedance and continuous power. Audio University walks through reading a QSC E115's nominal impedance (8 ohms) and continuous power rating from the manufacturer's technical specs, then matching it to an amplifier that supplies adequate power at that impedance. Power output is impedance-dependent: the same amplifier delivers more watts into a lower-impedance load, so the spec only means something when paired with an impedance. The video is honest about its own title. There is no simple rule, because any combination of equipment can cause damage under poor gain structure. The working recommendation that emerges is to choose an amplifier rated for about twice the speaker's continuous power, since a doubling of power is only a 3 dB change and the headroom keeps you from overdriving the amplifier input.

Questions

  1. 1.What two speaker specifications does the video say you need to check before choosing an amplifier, and where do you find them?Remember

    The two specs are nominal impedance, measured in ohms, and the power rating, measured in watts. Both are found in the speaker's technical specifications on the manufacturer's website. The power rating usually lists more than one figure, and the one that matters here is continuous power.

  2. 2.The video shows the GXD4 supplying 600 W at 4 ohms but only 400 W at 8 ohms. Explain in your own words why an amplifier's rated power output changes with the impedance of the speaker it drives.Understand

    Impedance is the opposition the speaker's circuit presents to current flow, and power output depends on how much current the amplifier can push through that load at a given voltage. A lower-impedance load draws more current, so the amplifier delivers more watts into it; a higher-impedance load draws less current and the amplifier delivers fewer watts. A power figure is meaningless without the impedance it was measured at, which is why amplifier specs always pair the two.

  3. 3.You have a pair of speakers with a nominal impedance of 8 ohms and a continuous power rating of 350 W each. Using the video's guideline, describe what to look for in an amplifier: how many channels, what power figure, and at what impedance that figure must be quoted.Apply

    The speakers are 8 ohms, so any amplifier power figure has to be quoted at 8 ohms to be comparable. Following the twice-continuous-power guideline, look for roughly 700 W per channel at 8 ohms. Because there are two speakers meant to run as a stereo pair, look for at least a two-channel amplifier so each speaker gets its own channel. Look for the headroom to tie back to gain structure: the extra capacity is there so the amplifier input is never overdriven.

  4. 4.The video argues that an underpowered amplifier is more likely to damage a speaker than an overpowered one. Walk through the reasoning behind that claim, and explain why "twice the continuous power rating" is offered as a guideline rather than a hard rule.Analyze

    An overpowered amplifier can exceed the speaker's limits, but the resulting distortion is audible, so the operator can hear it and turn down before damage occurs. An underpowered amplifier invites the opposite behavior: the operator keeps turning it up chasing level the amplifier cannot deliver, the amplifier clips, and the clipped waveform delivers sustained energy that overheats the voice coil without getting louder. The guideline is soft because the actual risk lives in gain structure, not in the spec sheet. Any pairing can be safe or dangerous depending on how it is operated, so twice the continuous rating is a sensible starting point for headroom.
