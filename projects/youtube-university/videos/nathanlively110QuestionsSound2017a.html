110 Questions about Sound System Tuning - #2 Kyra: What is sound system tuning?

Takeaway

A two-and-a-half-minute reframing of the basic question. Lively notes first that "tuning" is misleading shorthand: nobody is changing pitch, the work is sound system optimization. He then walks through what he used to think optimization meant (a handheld RTA, walk-around averaging, invert that average on a graphic EQ) and why it never produced good results, before pointing viewers to Bob McCarthy's principles of minimum variance as the framework that finally worked. The three principles get short, concrete glosses: level variance (not loud in front and quiet in back), spectral variance (not bright in front and dull in back), and appropriate sonic image (an actor upstage right should sound upstage right). The goal is the same sound for every audience member in the room.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Awareness that a PA produces uneven coverage across a room
  • Basic familiarity with RTA, graphic EQ, and frequency response as a concept

By the end you'll be able to

  • Restate the goal of sound system tuning as same-sound-everywhere rather than flat or good
  • Name the three principles of minimum variance and give an example of each
  • Explain why a handheld-RTA-and-invert workflow fails to deliver consistent results
