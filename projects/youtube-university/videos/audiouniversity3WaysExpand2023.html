Takeaway

Kyle walks through ADAT, MADI, and Dante by demonstrating each on real RME hardware (Babyface Pro FS, Digiface USB, Digiface Dante, 12Mic Dante) in a recording session context. The presentation organizes the three protocols by their topology constraints: ADAT is point-to-point optical with 8 channels per cable and a 5-meter reach. MADI is point-to-point or loop on coaxial cable with up to 64 channels and 100 m, 2 km, or 10 km reach depending on cable type. Dante is a star network with cross-point routing in Dante Controller and primary/secondary port redundancy. The middle of the video covers the often-skipped detail of ADAT word-clock direction (the demo audibly fails until the interface is set to use the preamp's clock). The closer applies the framework: the drum-room scenario rules out ADAT on distance, makes MADI viable on coax, and shows Dante as the most flexible option because devices just plug into the switch.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Familiarity with what a mic preamp does and where it sits in the signal chain
  • Awareness that audio interfaces have a limited number of physical inputs and outputs
  • Comfortable with the idea that digital audio can travel over many physical cable types

By the end you'll be able to

  • Compare the channel count, cable type, and distance limits of ADAT, MADI, and Dante
  • Diagnose an ADAT word-clock mismatch from audible symptoms and apply the clock-source fix
  • Distinguish point-to-point, loop, and star topologies and pick the right protocol for a given recording scenario
  • Identify how a Dante-equipped preamp or a MADI USB interface can bridge between protocols

Questions

  1. 1.The video distinguishes ADAT, MADI, and Dante by three properties: channel count per connection, cable type and distance, and topology. Summarize each protocol along those three axes.Understand

    ADAT: 8 channels per cable at 48 kHz (4 at 96 kHz, 2 at 192 kHz). Optical TOSLINK cable, up to 5 meters. Point-to-point: one device connects to one device per connection. MADI: 64 channels per cable at 48 kHz with the same channel-rate tradeoff. Coaxial cable to 100 m, multimode fiber to 2 km, single-mode fiber to 10 km. Point-to-point or loop topology (one cable carries audio in one direction, with devices chaining together for shared access to all 64 channels). Dante: channel count limited only by the network, well beyond what either ADAT or MADI carries. Standard Ethernet cabling (Cat5e or Cat6) at typical Ethernet distances per hop. Star topology centered on a network switch: every device connects to the switch and routes audio via Dante Controller's cross-point matrix.

  2. 2.You set up an ADAT connection between an interface and a preamp. The signal comes through but sounds wrong, with audible glitches and a kind of digital warble. The cable is good and the channels are correctly assigned. Apply the diagnostic Kyle demonstrates in the video. What is most likely the cause, and what is the fix?Apply

    The word-clock signal travels with the audio in one direction on a single ADAT cable. If the receiving interface is set to use its internal clock while the transmitting preamp is also sending its own clock through ADAT, the two clocks drift relative to each other and the receiver samples the incoming bit stream at the wrong instants. The result is the audible glitching Kyle demonstrates. The fix is to change the receiving interface's clock source from Internal to Optical (or whatever the device calls "clock from the ADAT input"), so it locks to the preamp's clock. After that, all eight channels sound clean.

  3. 3.Walk through Kyle's drum-recording scenario at the end of the video. The drummer is in an isolated room two floors up from the engineer. Why does this distance immediately rule out ADAT? Compare what MADI offers (the coax-cable solution) and Dante offers (the network-switch solution) for this specific setup, and identify when you would choose one over the other.Analyze

    The drummer is two floors up. ADAT is limited to about 5 meters (16 feet) over its TOSLINK optical cable, which is shorter than the run between floors. MADI on coaxial cable runs up to 100 m, more than enough. A single coax pair from a 12Mic Dante preamp by the drum kit to a Digiface Dante interface upstairs delivers all 12 mic channels, and a second cable returns a headphone mix to the drummer through the preamp's built-in headphone amp. Dante on Ethernet (still within the per-hop distance limit) does the same routing through Dante Controller, with the added flexibility of expanding the system later by plugging more devices into the switch without rewiring. MADI wins when the setup is simple and known (one preamp, one interface, one cable run). Dante wins when the system is likely to grow (more devices, more rooms, more sources) or when the engineer wants software-defined routing rather than physical patching.

  4. 4.The video shows that a single device can bridge between protocols: the RME 12Mic Dante can output MADI and Dante channels through its ADAT outputs, and a MADI USB interface can connect a non-Dante computer to a Dante network. Walk through one of these bridging scenarios in detail. What problem does protocol bridging solve, and what limitations (channel counts, clock sources) should an engineer still keep in mind?Analyze

    Bridging scenario: a studio has a 12Mic Dante preamp, a non-Dante USB audio interface (a Digiface USB with three ADAT inputs), and a separate computer running Dante Controller. The 12Mic Dante subscribes to up to 24 channels from a Dante network and routes them out its three ADAT outputs (8 per output). The Digiface USB receives all 24 ADAT channels and presents them to the DAW as inputs. Result: a non-Dante computer records 24 channels of Dante audio. The studio gains Dante's flexible routing without replacing its audio interface. Limitations: each ADAT connection caps at 8 channels at 48 kHz (and fewer at higher sample rates), so the 24-channel ceiling is fixed in hardware and cannot be changed in software. The master clock for the chain has to be coordinated (typically the 12Mic Dante locks to the Dante network's elected master and the Digiface USB locks to ADAT input clock), so misconfigured clocks reintroduce the problem Kyle demonstrates earlier in the video. Each protocol transition also adds a small latency that may matter in monitoring-critical applications.
