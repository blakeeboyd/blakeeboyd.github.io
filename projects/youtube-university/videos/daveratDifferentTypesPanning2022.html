Takeaway

Rat and his daughter Sammy (a speech-language pathology student) demonstrate four ways to pan a signal: level pan, Haas (delay) pan, EQ pan, and compression pan. They listen to each one on headphones and then through wide-spaced speakers, so the viewer can hear how the same technique can be drastic up close and subtle across a wide PA. Rat ties the technical content back to the practical question every live engineer has to answer: how do you get stereo imaging across a wide audience when most listeners only ever hear one PA hang clearly? Each technique behaves differently depending on whether the listener is on headphones, in a small venue near both speakers, or in a large venue near just one side.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Familiarity with the concept of stereo panning
  • Awareness that live venues differ from headphone listening
  • Some experience listening on both headphones and speakers

By the end you'll be able to

  • Distinguish four panning techniques: level, Haas, EQ, and compression
  • Explain why level panning fails in large venues
  • Describe how delay panning shifts perceived source location differently on headphones vs. wide-spaced speakers
  • Apply multiple panning cues simultaneously to create imaging without level changes

Watch for

  • 8:02 level panning demonstration: drastic on headphones, problematic across a wide PA
  • 8:35 Haas delay panning on headphones: drastic shift even with small delay
  • 10:55 same delay panning through speakers: much subtler, needs longer times
