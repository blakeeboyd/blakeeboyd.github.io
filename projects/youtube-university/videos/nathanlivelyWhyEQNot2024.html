Takeaway

The core argument: reach for EQ last, not first, when fighting feedback. Romney derives the math from JBL's Sound System Design Reference Manual: gain before feedback depends only on the four distances between talker, mic, loudspeaker, and listener, completely independent of how loud the talker is. From a starting case of 1 dB of gain, the live demo shows how moving the mic closer to the talker, getting the loudspeaker closer to the listener, and exploiting directivity (cardioid mic + 40° speaker) can yield 90+ dB of gain before feedback, all without touching an EQ. Closes with Q&A on mic cupping, polarity flip, decorrelation when the band stops playing, lavalier placement (anechoic chamber tests confirm: forehead beats chest), and how to push back when a client refuses headworn mics.

Before you watch

  • Basic understanding of microphone polar patterns (omnidirectional, cardioid, hypercardioid)
  • Comfortable following dB math or willing to follow it

By the end you'll be able to

  • Define acoustical gain and explain why feedback occurs at the level-equality threshold

  • Identify the four distances (D0, D1, D2, DS) that control gain before feedback and which to manipulate
  • Combine microphone and loudspeaker directivity to maximize gain without EQ
