Takeaway

Audio University compresses the working vocabulary of equalization into under three minutes. An EQ adjusts the frequency balance of a signal, and four concepts govern how: a frequency band is a range of frequencies, the center frequency sits in the middle of that range and is affected most, gain is the level change applied to the band, and bandwidth is how wide the adjustment reaches. A graphic EQ offers a fixed quantity of bands (commonly 31, centered on the third-octave ISO frequencies) and lets you change only their gain. A parametric EQ lets you set center frequency, gain, and bandwidth for each band. The host uses parametric in most cases for its precision and reaches for a graphic in some live situations, such as mixing stage monitors.

Questions

  1. 1.In your own words, define the four parameters the video introduces (frequency band, center frequency, gain, bandwidth) and explain how center frequency and bandwidth together describe the shape of an EQ adjustment.Understand

    Each term defines cleanly: a frequency band is a range of frequencies; the center frequency is the point in the middle of that range, which the adjustment affects most; gain is the amount the band is raised or lowered; bandwidth is how wide the adjustment spreads above and below the center. It should then connect center frequency and bandwidth: the center fixes where the adjustment happens and the bandwidth fixes how much of the surrounding spectrum comes along, so the two together describe the position and width of the curve.

  2. 2.A graphic EQ and a parametric EQ both let you boost or cut. Compare them on the three controllable parameters and explain what a parametric EQ can do that a graphic EQ cannot.Analyze

    A graphic EQ exposes only gain. Its bands sit at fixed center frequencies (the third-octave ISO set on a 31-band unit) and each band has a fixed bandwidth. A parametric EQ exposes all three: you sweep the center frequency, set the gain, and widen or narrow the bandwidth per band. The capability a parametric adds is targeting: you can place a band exactly on a problem frequency and tighten the bandwidth around it, rather than being limited to the nearest preset slider.

  3. 3.The host uses parametric EQ in most cases but chooses a graphic EQ for some live situations like stage monitors. Pick one task and justify which EQ type you would reach for and why.Apply

    The best responses commit to a task and tie the choice to the parameter tradeoff. For a stage monitor, a 31-band graphic gives a fast, visible layout where an engineer can pull down a feedback-prone band by feel without menu diving, which is why the host names it. For carving a narrow resonance out of a vocal, a parametric wins because you can sweep to the exact frequency and set a tight bandwidth. The reasoning should reference the precision-versus-speed tradeoff.
