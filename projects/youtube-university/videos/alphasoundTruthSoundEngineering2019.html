The Truth About Sound Engineering

Takeaway

A reflective video about how sound engineers should describe what they do. Sheets argues that engineers (and their clients) lack the language to describe what amplification actually does, which leaves the profession underappreciated and the classical world stuck in a defensive minimalist posture. His proposed framing: amplification is closer to photography or cinematography than to faithful reproduction. A great photograph is not a betrayal of the original scene; it's its own artistic object that uses the medium to do something the original cannot. Mic'ing a kick drum and pushing it through subwoofers works the same way. Once engineers can talk about their work in these terms, audiences can give the artistic choices the credit they currently miss.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Some live concert experience as listener or engineer
  • Awareness that amplification can be done well or poorly
  • Openness to thinking about audio as a creative medium

  • Identify the language gap that lets audiences miss engineers' contributions
  • Diagnose the classical world's minimalist stance toward amplification
  • Use the analogy to describe a specific moment where amplification added value beyond preservation
