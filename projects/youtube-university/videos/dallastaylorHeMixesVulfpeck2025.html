Takeaway

Jake Hartsfield, FOH for Vulfpeck, walks through his rig at a Red Rocks headlining show. The tour starts on stage with the mics, DI boxes, and the transformer-isolated 64-channel split that feeds both the monitor and FOH consoles (Allen and Heath dLive DM64s). It moves through in-ear monitors and the PSM 1000 transmitter chain, then over to FOH where he breaks down his vocal channel strip (gain, HPF/LPF, gate, insert, EQ, compressor, multi-band compressor), gain staging, the master bus into a matrix into the PA, and reverb/delay sends. He closes with how outdoor weather, stage reflections off Red Rocks, and audience density change what he hears mid-show. A complete signal-chain tour with a working engineer's commentary at every link.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Basic familiarity with what a mixing console does
  • Awareness of the difference between monitor and FOH mixing
  • Some exposure to channel strip elements (gain, EQ, compression)

By the end you'll be able to

  • Trace a signal from stage mic to PA through the live audio chain
  • Identify each stage of a vocal channel strip and what problem it solves
  • Explain why outdoor sound, audience density, and reflections change the engineer's tuning decisions
  • Describe what an FOH engineer manually does during a show vs. what automation handles

Watch for

  • 2:45 the transformer-isolated split that feeds both the monitor and FOH consoles
  • 9:30 Hartsfield's vocal channel strip walkthrough
  • 17:34 how audience density 'tightens up' the room. Hartsfield calls them 'meat bags'
