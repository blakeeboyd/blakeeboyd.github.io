Takeaway

Part four of Lively's reader Q&A series runs through twelve short answers on verification and placement. The polarity tip up top is a quick procedural recipe (auto adjust delay plus a solo near-field measurement of each driver), then the video shifts into placement, where the strongest material lives. Lively reframes ringing out the room as the last line of defense in a feedback fight, walking through the personal practice he developed of placing and aiming speakers for gain before feedback, then marking the feedback frequencies on an EQ as a backup he often never needs. The sub questions cover the central choices: hung versus ground stacked, two subs for power versus coverage versus directivity, the two-thirds wavelength rule for keeping multiple subs within summing distance, and the boundary-as-mirror reason a cardioid sub array fails under a stage. He closes with the line that anchors the placement chapter: once acoustic comb filtering takes 12 dB out, no EQ puts it back.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Familiarity with system tuning terminology (verification, polarity, delay, ringing out)
  • Basic awareness of microphone polar patterns and feedback
  • Exposure to subwoofer configurations (stacked, flown, cardioid)

By the end you'll be able to

  • Describe a quick polarity verification using auto adjust delay and a near-field solo measurement
  • Explain why improving setup geometry yields more gain before feedback than ringing out the room
  • Choose a two-sub configuration based on whether the goal is power, even coverage, or directivity
  • Justify when EQ can correct an interference problem and when only physical changes can
