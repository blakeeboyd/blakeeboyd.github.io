What Running a Small Live Sound Company Looks Like

Silver Creek Audio · ~10:30 · Applied

Live Sound / Sound Reinforcement, Soundcheck, FOH Mixing

microphonessignal-flowgain-structure

Watch on YouTube ↗

Takeaway

Connor of Silver Creek Audio documents a full small-production day: two artists he has never met, a van to unload, a PA to build, and a show to mix solo. The video moves through load-in, raising the speakers on tilter poles, a mic check, meeting Bri Bagwell and Paul Eason, and a soundcheck where he gets a rough mix from the mains and lets the artists get comfortable. It closes with Bri Bagwell performing "Roof Over My Head" through the finished system. The video shows the entry-level workflow without dramatizing it: one person, every job, no crew.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • General awareness of what a PA system is and what it does
  • No console or mixing experience required
  • Curiosity about live sound as a working trade

By the end you'll be able to

  • Describe the stages of a small live production from load-in to downbeat
  • Explain what a soundcheck accomplishes and what it deliberately leaves unfinished
  • Identify why an engineer carries redundant gear that often goes unused
  • Recognize the constraints that distinguish one-person small-venue work from large-scale production

Watch for

  • 0:00 the production overview: two artists he has never met, a van to unload, a PA to build solo
  • 6:38 soundcheck goals stated plainly: get a rough mix from the mains, check the lights, let the artists settle in
  • 7:35 Bri Bagwell performs 'Roof Over My Head' through the finished system

Questions

  1. 1.Connor moves through the full arc of a small singer-songwriter gig: load-in, PA setup, mic check, soundcheck, show. Summarize what happens at each stage and what he is trying to have in place by the time the show starts.Understand
  2. 2.During soundcheck Connor gets "a rough mix coming out of the mains" and says he can "get it going once we start the show." Put yourself in his position. What can and cannot be finalized before an audience is in the room, and why is it worth leaving room to adjust?Apply
  3. 3.Connor puts spare microphones and DI boxes on stage even though performers usually bring their own gear and rarely touch his. Walk through the trade-off he is managing. What does carrying that redundancy cost him, and what does it protect against?Analyze
  4. 4.This is a one-person operation: two unfamiliar artists, a van of gear, no second engineer. Identify the constraints that scale creates here, and contrast them with the working conditions of a festival FOH engineer.Analyze
