#4 Is sound system tuning without software really possible?

Takeaway

A one-minute snippet from Lively's 110 Questions series. Hal asks whether tuning without software is really possible or whether the engineer is just shooting for good enough. Lively answers with an analogy: a doctor trapped in an elevator with a woman about to give birth doesn't want to be there, but still does the job. The analyzer is the preferred tool every time, but a missing analyzer doesn't stop the work. The video serves as a one-minute counterpoint to Devin Sheets's argument that by-ear is the primary method and the analyzer is the guest-engineer accommodation.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Awareness that measurement software (Smaart, Open Sound Meter) is the industry-standard system tuning tool
  • Familiarity with the basic claim that PA systems need tuning when moved between venues

By the end you'll be able to

  • Paraphrase Lively's elevator analogy and identify what it implies about tool dependence in professional practice
  • Take a position on whether tuning without software is a fallback or a legitimate first-line method

Watch for

  • 0:39 the analogy lands: even with the analyzer in his bag, the job still gets done without it
