Live Sound Mixing Strategy - Updated

Dave Rat · 15:55 · Applied

Takeaway

Rat shares the routing strategy he has been using since the late 1980s to mix rock shows. He sends everything into six instrument-grouped subgroups (vocals, guitars, bass, kick-snare, toms, cymbals), puts a compressor on each group with the threshold at 0 dB on the instruments and +4 dB on the vocals, and lets the compression ratios force the vocals on top without intervention. He then assigns two VCAs across the board: VCA 10 controls the input faders (pre-compressor) and VCA 11 controls the group faders (post-compressor). Pushing one up while pulling the other down changes how compressed the mix sounds without changing the audience volume, which lets him swing between dense, contained sound for fast songs and open, dynamic sound for slow ones in real time. The goal is to mix without micromanaging: focus on the band, the audience, and the sound, not the console.

Questions

  1. 1.Rat groups all inputs into six instrument categories, puts a compressor on each group, and sets the threshold at 0 dB on instruments but +4 dB on vocals. Explain what this does to the vocal-to-instrument balance when the band gets louder, and why he calls this an "automix."Analyze

    With the band at moderate volume, every instrument hits its 0 dB threshold and gets compressed; the vocals at +4 dB threshold are below their threshold so they pass uncompressed. When the band gets louder (instruments rising above 0 dB), each instrument's compressor reduces gain, but the vocal compressor stays inactive until the vocals hit +4 dB. So as the band gets louder, the instruments get more compressed relative to the vocals. The vocal-to-instrument ratio actually improves automatically. Rat calls this an "automix" because it does the level-balancing work without engineer intervention: as long as everything is roughly gain-matched at the threshold, the ratios self-correct as dynamics change.

  2. 2.Why does Rat deliberately not stereo-link the compressors on his stereo subgroups? Walk through the example of a guitar mix that is louder on the left than the right, and explain what would happen with linked vs. unlinked compression on that pair.Analyze

    If the guitar mix is louder on the left than the right (perhaps because the left amp is louder or one rig sounds bigger), a linked stereo compressor would respond to the combined L+R signal, pulling both sides down by the same amount and preserving the left-heavy balance. The mix is more compressed, but still louder on the left. Unlinked compression processes each side independently: the left side hits its threshold first and compresses more, the right side compresses less, and the balance gets pulled toward center. Rat wants the compressor to moderate the imbalance, not preserve it, so he leaves them unlinked. The unlinked approach also preserves stereo imaging for stereo content (like panned cymbal crashes), which linked compression would collapse to mono whenever the dynamics opened.

  3. 3.Rat builds his "compression VCA" trick using VCA 10 on inputs (pre-compressor) and VCA 11 on groups (post-compressor). Describe what happens to the mix when he pushes VCA 10 up and pulls VCA 11 down, and what happens when he reverses it. Why is this useful as a real-time tool during a show?Apply

    VCA 10 up + VCA 11 down: more signal hits each compressor's threshold (more gain reduction overall), then less of that compressed signal goes to the PA (same overall loudness). Net result: same volume, mix is more squeezed (more compression, less dynamic range, denser sound). Reversed: less signal hits thresholds (less compression), more of that less-compressed signal goes to the PA. Same volume, more dynamic, more open, more transient detail. Useful as a real-time tool because Rat can switch between dense-and-loud (fast aggressive songs) and open-and-detailed (slow songs, ballads, breakdowns) by moving two faders without changing audience volume.

  4. 4.Rat routes vocal effects (reverb, delay) into the same vocal subgroup as the dry vocals, instead of returning them to the main bus. What problem does this solve? What would go wrong if the effects bypassed the vocal group's compressor?Analyze

    If the vocal effects (reverb, delay) return to the main bus while the dry vocals route through the vocal subgroup, the vocals compress dynamically (loud notes get pulled down) but the effects don't. The effects stay at their original level. As the dry vocal compresses, the effects rise relative to it; eventually the effects swamp the dry vocal. Routing the effects into the same vocal subgroup means everything passes through the vocal compressor together. Dry and wet move as a unit, the dry-to-wet ratio stays constant regardless of vocal level. The vocal as a sonic entity (dry + effects) remains coherent.
