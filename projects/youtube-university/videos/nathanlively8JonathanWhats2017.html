Takeaway

Lively answers Jonathan's question by sketching a time-budget ladder for tuning. 60 seconds buys output verification. 5 minutes adds a measurement-mic snapshot for later reference. 15 minutes adds main-array EQ, level, and sub-to-main phase alignment. 30 minutes lets you start matching subsystems in delay, level, and EQ. Real life usually spends part of any budget fixing a broken speaker before any optimization happens, which is itself an argument for doing the 60-second output check first.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Familiarity with the basic system tuning vocabulary (output verification, EQ, level, delay, phase alignment)
  • Awareness that a measurement microphone plus analyzer can produce a visual response curve
  • Some exposure to the difference between a main array and subsystems (subs, fills, delays)

By the end you'll be able to

  • Recall the order of operations Lively recommends as time allowances grow from 60 seconds to 30 minutes
  • Apply the 'main array wins by default' heuristic to prioritize a tuning move when time is short
  • Distinguish the hypothetical clean scenario from the realistic one where technical problems consume part of the time budget
