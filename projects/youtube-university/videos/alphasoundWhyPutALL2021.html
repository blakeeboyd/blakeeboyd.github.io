Why put ALL your subwoofers on one side??

Alpha Sound · 03:33 · Applied

Live Sound / System Design, System Tuning

subscomb-filtering

Watch on YouTube ↗

Takeaway

Sheets answers a recurring question with a short applied argument. The ideal is a mono center cluster of subs in front of the stage, but a stage banner blocks that placement at this venue, so all the subs go on one side instead. The hidden cost of the alternative, a stereo sub split, is that the broadband prediction looks smooth while a per-frequency view shows massive nulls and cancellation across most of the audience. The mono-side rig gives a uniform frequency response everywhere; the price is an SPL gradient that runs about 9 dB front-row side-to-side, 3 dB midway back, and nothing at the back wall. Sheets argues that listeners who do not walk the venue have no reference for comparison, and that bass is spatially ambiguous enough to bind psychoacoustically to the well-imaged mid-range above it.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Familiar with the idea that two coherent sources produce comb filtering
  • Aware that broadband prediction software can hide per-frequency problems

By the end you'll be able to

  • Distinguish broadband and per-frequency views of a stereo sub prediction
  • Explain why an asymmetric SPL distribution can be a better trade than comb filtering
  • Decide between mono-side and stereo sub placement given a venue constraint

Watch for

  • 2:25 why off-center bass is acceptable: low frequencies are spatially ambiguous and psychoacoustically bound to upper harmonics
