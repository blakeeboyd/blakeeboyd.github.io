Takeaway

David Poirier, lead audio tech for Cirque du Soleil's "Drawn to Life," walks through how the show is mixed live with music that follows the acrobatics rather than a click track. He covers the VCA-based console layout that lets him drop any musician onto pre-recorded tracks at a moment's notice, the multi-array Meyer LEOPARD PA with cardioid sub configurations, the use of Meyer D-Mitri to triangulate sound across three speakers instead of stereo-panning between two, and the Constellation/VRAS system that captures audience sound through 15 hidden mics and pumps it back through the PA to amplify laughter and applause. He ties the technical layout to his mixing approach: dynamic, performer-attentive, and willing to pull music back when stage communication needs to come through.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Basic familiarity with live mixing or theater audio
  • Awareness that PAs can be configured in stereo, 5.1, or immersive formats
  • Curiosity about how sound design supports performance

By the end you'll be able to

  • Explain how Meyer D-Mitri's triangulation differs from traditional stereo panning
  • Describe how VCA-based track substitution lets a musician's part be replaced live
  • Articulate the aesthetic and risks of audience-augmentation systems like Constellation/VRAS
  • Identify situations where the engineer prioritizes performer safety over sound design
