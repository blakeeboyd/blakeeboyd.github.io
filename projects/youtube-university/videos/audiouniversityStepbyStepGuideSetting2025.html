Takeaway

Audio University walks through setting up a small live sound system as six ordered steps, demonstrated on a Mackie SRM rig with a ProFX12 mixer. The ordering is the point: plan the room layout first (speaker placement, mixer location, cable pathways) so that decisions about coverage, feedback, and tripping hazards are settled before any cable is run. Then verify power, normal the mixer to a blank slate, connect outputs, connect inputs, and only at the end set gain and route signals. The gain-staging procedure at the end is where the video says most people go wrong: speakers down, main fader at Unity, PFL each input to about 0 dB, then bring faders to Unity so mixing happens in the fader's most controllable range. It closes with a feedback troubleshooting ladder, from moving the mic relative to the speakers up to engaging a high pass filter and notching with subtractive EQ.

Before you watch

Prerequisites

  • Familiarity with the basic components of a small PA: mixer, mains, subs, monitors
  • Awareness that microphones, instruments, and line devices connect through different inputs
  • Comfortable with the idea of signal flow from input through mixer to speaker

By the end you'll be able to

  • Explain why room layout, power, and connections are sequenced before any gain is set
  • Apply the gain-staging procedure: speakers down, main fader at Unity, PFL each input to 0 dB, faders to Unity
  • Analyze the three subwoofer connection options and what control each one gives up or gains
  • Walk through the feedback troubleshooting ladder from speaker placement to a subtractive EQ notch

Watch for

  • 1:11 the placement rule that prevents feedback: keep stage microphones behind the speakers, not in front of them
  • 5:31 the subwoofer high pass out as a built-in crossover between subs and mains
  • 9:56 the gain-staging sequence begins: speakers all the way down, main fader to Unity before any channel comes up
