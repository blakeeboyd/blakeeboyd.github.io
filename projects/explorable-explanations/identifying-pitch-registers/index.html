Purpose

This exercize is designed to further the transition from analytical to critical evaluation. In Chapter 4, Moylan argues that the industry often relies on imprecise terms like "muddy" or "crisp" which rely on personal associations and fail to communicate meaninful information as a result. Learning to speak about the physical dimentions of a sound source can assist you in presenting clear and consice communication with your clients and colleagues.

As with previous exercizes, you will learn pink noise to learn the characteristics of a frequency band followed by a musical example. Using the reference track you chose in Week 2 is reccomended.

Task

Use the explorable above to undertake a two-stage listening evaluation. Please follow the steps below in order.

Step 1: Calibration (Pink Noise)

Before analyzing your reference track, calibrate your ears to the sound of each frequency band using pink noise.

  1. Select "Pink Noise" in the tool. Adjust the volume until the sound is at a comfortable volume.
  2. Solo each available frequency band one at a time.
  3. Listen and define: For each band, take a moment to internalize the physical characteristic of that specific range. Ask yourself: Is there a resonance or a formant I associate with what I am hearing?

Note: Use this step to remind yourself of the raw sound of the frequency band without the distraction of musical information. This creates the "reference experience" Moylan identifies as crucial for evaluation.

Step 2: Application (Musical Analysis)

Switch the tool to "User Audio" and upload a track of your choice. Isolate a loop no longer than 30 seconds in the song that contains instruments across the full spectrum. Perform the same isolation process for each frequency band and answer the following questions:

  1. What instruments do you hear in the audio example?
  2. In each band, what instruments are present?
  3. For instruments that appear in multiple bands, in which specific band is the instrument most present or dominant?

After completing the steps above, enable the filters with all of the bands in (none soloed or muted). Toggle the filters on and off and write a short description of the difference between the two states. What instruments or areas of the spectrum are affected? This website uses filters with extremely hard cutoffs but there is still an audible remnant of them.

Criteria

Your analysis will be evaluated based on the following:

      Do you avoid the "intersensory" adjectives (warm, bright, crisp) warned against in the text, and instead identify the source based on physical characteristics?
      Have you correctly identified which instruments occupy the specific frequency bands?
      Did you complete the calibration step? Your notes should reflect a comparison between the raw pink noise energy and the resulting musical content.
